Foundations · Obsidian vault

A meteorology course, rebuilt as a graph

Ten chapters, 324 single-concept notes, 2,654 wikilinks. Every note links up to its chapter and across to the concepts it depends on, so the graph view shows the whole course as connected clusters.

How it's organized

  • Hub and spokes. A home note indexes ten chapter notes; each chapter indexes its concept notes; each concept links to prerequisites and to what it unlocks.
  • One concept per note, with chapter tags (e.g. #MeteorologyChapter5) so the graph can be filtered or colored per chapter.
  • Summaries per chapter in plain language, with key numbers collected in one table.
  • Corrections are logged, not silently overwritten, and the original notes are kept as a backup.
  • Applied layer: a CM1 namelist reference and a research-ideas note sit beside the course notes so theory and modeling live in one graph.

The notes are my own study material and are not published here; this page shows the structure and the parts I use most.

Most-used reference

Convective parameters

The Chapter 5 set I rely on when building soundings for CM1 and when reading storm environments.

CAPE: buoyant energy between the LFC and EL $$\mathrm{CAPE}=g\int_{z_{\mathrm{LFC}}}^{z_{\mathrm{EL}}}\frac{T_{v,\mathrm{parcel}}-T_{v,\mathrm{env}}}{T_{v,\mathrm{env}}}\,dz\quad[\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}}]$$
QuantityWhat it measuresWhy it matters
CAPEEnergy a parcel gains from positive buoyancy between LFC and EL (J/kg); on a Skew-T, the area where the lifted parcel is warmer than the environmentMore CAPE → stronger updrafts → more intense storms
CINEnergy needed to lift a parcel to the LFCOpposes CAPE; a "cap" delays or prevents initiation
LCL / LFC / ELLifting condensation level / level of free convection / equilibrium levelCloud base, onset of free convection, approximate storm top
Bulk wind shearVector wind difference across a layerOrganizes updrafts and sustains storms
Storm-relative helicityStreamwise vorticity available to a stormRotation potential, especially in the lowest 1–3 km
Supercell compositeCombines CAPE, helicity and shearScreens for supercell environments
Significant tornado parameterCombines CAPE, LCL height, 0–1 km SRH, bulk shear and CINValues ≥ 1 typically favor EF2+ tornadoes; formulation varies (fixed-layer vs effective-layer)
Why HSLC is awkward. Composite indices weight CAPE heavily, so environments with little CAPE but strong shear can score low even when they produce damaging QLCS winds or tornadoes. That gap is the reason my modeling plan focuses on that regime (see NWP lab).
